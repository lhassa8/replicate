Replicate began with a factory-floor idea: hundreds of connected machines, each with modest compute, sharing the ability to run intelligence locally. New machines would receive the current inference engine from authorized peers; a self-healing mesh would distribute approved updates and recover from failures without relying on a central orchestrator. The paper demonstrates a first step: bounded software self-replication across prepared devices, including a descendant that reproduces after the original seed is removed. Three lineages completed six births. A separate fixed-rule control also succeeded without model calls, so replication success does not establish a need for model reasoning. The larger mesh, update, and recovery capabilities remain goals for future testing.

Paper and source: https://github.com/lhassa8/replicate
