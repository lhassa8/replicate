Abstract:
We demonstrate bounded self-replication of a complete, locally inferring software agent across owner-prepared receivers. A parent supplies the signed software package and local model; its child repeats the operation after the seed disappears. The Replicate testbed makes package provenance, local inference, useful operation, and ancestor independence explicit and auditable. On prepared 8 GiB x86-64 Linux guests, three seed-child-grandchild lineages using a fine-tuned 3.66-billion-parameter model completed six births. The two surviving descendants of the final trial performed periodic work for two hours. Across the native gate and lineages, all 260 executions of reused development diagnostics passed; the largest observed service-group memory was 5.001 GiB. A separately frozen, cohosted comparison reconstructed two births and 15/15 diagnostics for both trained and deterministic policies. Fixed policy used zero model calls; stock passed five seed diagnostics but submitted no executable reproduction action. On forty post-development synthetic cases, trained and fixed policies passed 40/40, versus 32/40 for stock. Separate-code auditors accepted 24/24 valid controls and rejected 108/108 prescribed record inconsistencies. We retain failed attempts and supply source-bound records, model assets, and replay procedures. The results establish replication feasibility and fixed-policy sufficiency within this prepared task, separating successful replication from the necessity of model reasoning. They do not establish general autonomous replication or population reliability.
